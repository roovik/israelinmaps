HOT · History

Why did about 700,000 Palestinians leave in 1948, and may they return?

Contested issue · NOWlej's view · As of 21 October 2026

In brief

About 700,000 Arabs left their homes in the territory that became Israel during the war of 1947 to 1949, and Israel did not let them return. The war began when the Arab states and Palestinian Arab leaders rejected the UN partition plan and attacked. NOWlej's view: the great majority left because of a war the Arab side started, and in a few places Israeli forces ordered residents out, but there was no plan to expel a people. The refusal to readmit them is the heart of today's dispute.

What critics claim

  • Palestinians call 1948 the Nakba, the catastrophe. They say that most Palestinian Arabs were expelled or terrorized into leaving, that villages were destroyed to prevent return, and that the refugees and their descendants have a right to return under UN General Assembly Resolution 194.
  • Some historians argue that Plan Dalet, a Haganah plan of March 1948, was meant to clear Arab areas. Others read it as a military plan to secure roads to besieged Jewish towns and Jerusalem.

What the record shows

  • The UN General Assembly voted for partition on 29 November 1947. The Jewish leadership accepted it. The Arab Higher Committee and the Arab states rejected it.
  • Fighting began at once. Five Arab armies entered on 15 May 1948, the day after Israel declared independence.
  • Historians agree that about 700,000 Arabs left, in waves, and that many left before the Arab armies invaded.
  • Historians, including Israelis such as Benny Morris, document that in some places, among them Lydda and Ramle in July 1948, Israeli forces ordered residents out. In many places residents fled the fighting itself. Historians dispute how much weight each cause deserves.
  • Israel did not let the refugees return, and abandoned property was taken over. Resolution 194 of December 1948 said refugees wishing to live in peace with their neighbors should be permitted to return at the earliest practicable date. It is a General Assembly recommendation and is not binding.
  • Roughly 800,000 Jews left or were forced out of Arab countries in the decades after 1948, and most settled in Israel.

The case and the answers

The critics' case

  • Whatever caused the flight, Israel's policy afterward was to bar return and to settle new immigrants on Arab lands, which makes the outcome permanent by design.
  • A people cannot lose its homes because of a war. The refugees' descendants now number in the millions and remain stateless in many places.

NOWlej's answer

  • The war began when the Arab side rejected partition and invaded. Refugees are the usual result of a war, as with millions of Germans after the Second World War and the millions moved in the partition of India. Only the Palestinian refugees were never resettled by the Arab states that took the lead in the war.
  • No state is obliged to admit a large population from the side that fought against its creation. Resolution 194 is conditioned on living in peace and is not binding.
  • Israel took in roughly 800,000 Jewish refugees from Arab lands. Most Arab states refused Palestinian refugees citizenship, and the UN agency for them, UNRWA, alone counts descendants as refugees.
  • A return of millions of descendants would end the Jewish majority, which is why most Israelis hear the demand as a call to end the Jewish state.

Key dates

  • 29 Nov 1947 UN General Assembly votes to partition Mandatory Palestine.
  • Mar 1948 Plan Dalet is drawn up by the Haganah.
  • 9 Apr 1948 Deir Yassin, where Irgun and Lehi fighters killed villagers, spreads fear across the country.
  • 14 May 1948 Israel declares independence and Arab armies invade.
  • Jul 1948 Lydda and Ramle are taken and most of their residents leave or are forced out.
  • 11 Dec 1948 UN General Assembly adopts Resolution 194.
  • 1949 Armistice agreements end the fighting. Israel refuses return.
