HISTORICAL MAPS

MAP 22 Judea and Samaria

Areas A, B and C under the Oslo Accords

The northern and southern heartland regions of the Land of Israel carry deep historical and spiritual significance, with roots in Biblical times. They hold sites central to Jewish tradition, such as Hebron, home to the Cave of the Patriarchs and Matriarchs, and Shiloh, the ancient site of the Tabernacle. After the Six-Day War in 1967 the Jewish population grew sharply as communities were reestablished.

Judea and Samaria, which the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan annexed in 1950 in a step that few countries recognized, are a focal point of conflict. Disputes over land, governance and sovereignty often escalate into violence and terror.

Repeated efforts at peace, including the 1993 Oslo Accords and later proposals, have failed each time because the Arab side refused compromises recognizing Israel as the national homeland of the Jewish people.

The territories remain under overall Israeli security control, with the Palestinian Authority administering the Arab population in designated autonomous zones. The 1995 Oslo II agreement divided the territory into Areas A, B and C.

PRINCIPAL PLACES

Hebron About 200,000. Cave of the Patriarchs and Matriarchs, and King David’s first capital.

Kiryat Arba About 8,000. Israeli town beside Hebron, founded in 1968.

Shechem (Nablus) About 160,000. Where Abraham built his first altar. Joseph’s Tomb stands here.

Bethlehem About 25,000. Rachel’s Tomb, birthplace of King David, and the Church of the Nativity.

Jericho About 21,000. One of the oldest cities in the world, entered by Joshua.

Ramallah About 40,000. Administrative seat of the Palestinian Authority.

Shiloh Israeli community. Site of the Tabernacle in the days of the Judges.

Ariel About 20,000. Israeli city in the heart of Samaria, home to Ariel University.

Maale Adumim About 38,000. Israeli city east of Jerusalem, founded in 1975.

Efrat and Gush Etzion About 12,000 in Efrat. Gush Etzion fell in 1948 and was rebuilt after 1967.

Israeli residents 541,085 at the end of 2025, not counting east Jerusalem.
