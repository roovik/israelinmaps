HISTORICAL MAPS

MAP 7 Jewish Communities

7th to 11th centuries

After Emperor Julian II’s death in 363 CE, Jewish settlement in the south declined under imperial policy, war and economic hardship. Jewish life endured in the Galilee and in cities such as Tiberias, Sepphoris and Caesarea. Tiberias became a center of scholarship, where the Masoretic Text of the Hebrew Bible was developed.

The Byzantine era alternated oppression with relative autonomy. After the Muslim conquest in the 7th century, Jews, as dhimmis, received a limited but protected status in exchange for a tax, and trade, agriculture and crafts revived.

Jerusalem kept a small but influential Jewish population. Under Muslim rule Jews again had access to the Temple Mount, which Byzantine governance had denied.
