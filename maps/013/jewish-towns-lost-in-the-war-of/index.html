HISTORICAL MAPS

MAP 13 Jewish Towns Lost in the War of Independence

1947 to 1948

During the War of Independence in 1947 and 1948, Jewish communities were destroyed or abandoned. The ancient Jewish community of Hebron fled as hostilities grew, ending a presence dating back millennia. North of the Dead Sea, Kibbutz Beit Ha’arava and Kalya were captured by the Jordanian army, displacing their residents. Four kibbutzim in Gush Etzion, west of Bethlehem, fell after fierce battles, marking the loss of pioneering settlements. North of Jerusalem, Atarot and Neve Yaakov, key Jewish outposts, were also overtaken. The Jewish Quarter of Jerusalem’s Old City was captured, with its residents exiled and synagogues desecrated. In the Gaza Strip, Kfar Darom was seized by the Egyptian army after prolonged resistance.

The Jewish Quarter of Jerusalem’s Old City, a historic and spiritual center for Jews, faced devastating loss. After weeks of siege by the Jordanian Arab Legion, the quarter fell in May 1948. The Jewish defenders, outnumbered and outgunned, were forced to surrender. The residents were expelled, and centuries-old synagogues, including the iconic Hurva Synagogue, were systematically destroyed or desecrated. The Jewish Quarter remained under Jordanian control, inaccessible to Jews, until Israel’s reunification of Jerusalem in 1967.
